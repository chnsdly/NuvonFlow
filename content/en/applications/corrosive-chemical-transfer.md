## Application context

Corrosive chemical transfer lines can involve acids, alkalis, oxidizers, solvents, salt solutions, mixed streams, or cleaning chemicals. The selection challenge is rarely pressure rating alone. Media concentration, temperature, exposure time, velocity, outdoor conditions, cleaning method, and maintenance practice can all change the acceptable material set.

The strongest RFQ describes the transfer path, not just a product name. It should show where the chemical comes from, where it goes, how the line is isolated, how it is drained or flushed, and which documents are needed for project review.

## Typical duty conditions

| Process area | Common flow control role | Information to confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bulk unloading and transfer | Isolation, pump protection, emergency shutoff, and drain points | Chemical, concentration, transfer rate, hose or pipe connection, containment |
| Day tanks and feed lines | Tank outlet isolation, recirculation, check protection, and sample points | Tank material, suction conditions, pump data, valve orientation |
| Process feed or neutralization | Controlled isolation, chemical compatibility, flushing, and batch handling | Temperature, pressure, exposure time, cleaning method, solids or reaction byproducts |
| Outdoor or exposed piping | Corrosion-resistant materials, UV/weather review, and safe maintenance | Ambient exposure, supports, fasteners, labeling, inspection requirements |

## Product families usually reviewed

[Ball valves](/products/ball-valves/) are common for clean chemical isolation when the media and solids profile allow it. [Diaphragm valves](/products/diaphragm-valves/) are often reviewed when containment, dirty service, or aggressive media are central concerns. [Check valves](/products/check-valves/) protect pumps and help prevent cross-contamination. [Fittings](/products/fittings/), gaskets, fasteners, and adapters should be reviewed as part of the same wetted system. [Actuators](/products/actuators/) may improve safety when remote operation or interlocks are required.

## Selection risks to clarify

- Chemical name alone is not enough; concentration, temperature, impurities, and cleaning chemicals matter.
- Mixed-material systems may fail at seals, gaskets, fasteners, liners, or adapters even when the main valve body is compatible.
- Vacuum, intermittent drying, crystallization, thermal expansion, or outdoor exposure can change the recommendation.
- Transfer lines may require secondary containment, drain points, flush points, lockout expectations, and clear labeling.

## RFQ preparation

Provide chemical name, concentration, temperature, pressure, flow rate, transfer distance, pipe material, connection standard, valve function, pump information, installation orientation, containment or flushing requirements, quantity, and required documents. If the line handles more than one chemical, list each credible exposure.
