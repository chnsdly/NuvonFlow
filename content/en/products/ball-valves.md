## Where ball valves fit in a system

Ball valves are commonly selected when a line needs compact, fast on/off isolation. In corrosion-resistant piping systems, they are often considered for clean water, treated water, chemical feed lines, dosing skids, tank outlets, sample points, utility headers, and maintenance bypasses.

For most corrosive-service projects, a ball valve is not selected by the valve name alone. The body material, ball and stem design, seat material, seal set, end connection, pressure-temperature boundary, operating frequency, and actuator interface should all be reviewed against the actual duty.

## Typical service situations

| Service area | Why ball valves are considered | Review points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chemical dosing lines | Compact isolation around tanks, pumps, calibration columns, and injection assemblies | Chemical concentration, tubing or pipe standard, pressure, leak-tightness expectation |
| Water treatment skids | Manual or actuated shutoff in packaged equipment | Space, connection style, cycling frequency, actuator clearance |
| Sampling and drain points | Simple isolation for small lines and service points | Operator access, lockout need, discharge safety |
| Corrosive transfer lines | Isolation for compatible clean chemicals | Body, seat, seal, static charge, temperature, and pressure |

## Selection notes

Ball valves are best reviewed as shutoff valves. They may be used in partially open positions only when the design and service allow it; ordinary ball valves are not usually the first choice for throttling. If modulation or frequent position control is required, clarify the duty before assuming a standard on/off valve is suitable.

Material compatibility should include every wetted part. The body material may be PVC, CPVC, PP, PVDF, lined metal, stainless steel, or another project-specific option, but the seat and seal materials can be equally important. PTFE seats are common in many chemical services, while elastomers must be checked against the media, concentration, and temperature.

For automated ball valves, confirm valve torque, safety factor, actuator supply, fail position, cycle frequency, local override, limit switch, solenoid, and enclosure expectations. A compact valve can still become difficult to install if actuator clearance is not reviewed early.

## Common documents to request

Depending on the project, the quotation or submittal package may need:

- Product datasheet
- Dimensional drawing
- Pressure-temperature information by product series
- Material declaration or wetted-part summary
- IOM or maintenance instructions
- Actuator wiring or pneumatic accessory information
- Inspection or test documentation when required by the purchase scope

## RFQ checklist

To prepare a more reviewable inquiry, include:

- Pipe size or DN/NPS
- Body material preference
- Seat and seal material preference, if known
- Chemical name, concentration, temperature, and pressure
- End connection and pipe standard
- Manual handle, lockable handle, pneumatic actuator, or electric actuator requirement
- Normal position, fail position, and cycle frequency if automated
- Quantity, destination country, and target documents

Final material suitability and pressure-temperature limits should be confirmed against the actual product series and project duty before purchase.
