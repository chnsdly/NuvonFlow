## Where butterfly valves fit in a system

Butterfly valves are often selected for larger pipe sizes where a compact, relatively lightweight valve is preferred for isolation or basic flow control. They are common in industrial water treatment, wastewater treatment, cooling water, utility headers, pretreatment systems, filtration lines, and selected chemical service where the liner, disc, and shaft materials match the duty.

Butterfly valves can be simple to install, but they are not simple to specify. The pipe standard, flange standard, valve style, liner material, disc material, shaft sealing, operating mode, and allowable leakage expectation should be reviewed before quotation.

## Typical service situations

| Service area | Why butterfly valves are considered | Review points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water treatment headers | Larger sizes, compact operation, manual or actuated isolation | Pipe/flange standard, pressure, liner material, actuator torque |
| Wastewater and sludge-adjacent lines | Compact valves for variable service conditions | Solids, fouling risk, liner abrasion, shaft sealing |
| Chemical utility systems | Isolation or basic control where compatible materials are available | Media, concentration, temperature, disc and liner compatibility |
| Automated process lines | Remote operation for sequencing, backwash, or process control | Actuator type, control signal, cycle frequency, fail position |

## Selection notes

Confirm pipe and flange standards early. Wafer, lug, double-flange, and grooved styles are not interchangeable, and the wrong body style can create installation or maintenance problems. Lug bodies may be preferred where downstream piping removal or end-of-line service must be considered, subject to product design and pressure limits.

For corrosive or wastewater service, the liner and disc cannot be reviewed separately from the shaft, bushings, fasteners, and sealing details. A suitable body coating is not enough if the wetted elastomer or disc material is not compatible with the media.

If the valve will be used for throttling or modulation, clarify the control range, flow stability, expected leakage, and cycle frequency. Some butterfly valves are suitable for basic control, while others are better treated as isolation valves.

## Common documents to request

Depending on the project, request:

- Product datasheet
- Face-to-face and flange dimensional drawing
- Material and liner information
- Pressure-temperature information by product series
- Actuator sizing or accessory information
- IOM or maintenance instructions
- Inspection, test, or packing documentation when required

## RFQ checklist

To prepare a more reviewable inquiry, include:

- DN/NPS size and pipe standard
- Flange standard and preferred body style
- Media, concentration, temperature, and pressure
- Body, disc, stem, and liner material preference, if known
- Manual lever, gear, pneumatic, or electric operation
- Control signal, fail position, and cycle frequency if actuated
- Installation orientation and whether the valve is for isolation or throttling
- Quantity, destination, drawing needs, and inspection requirements

Final valve style, pressure-temperature limit, leakage expectation, and material suitability should be confirmed against the selected product series and the actual service conditions.
