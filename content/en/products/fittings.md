## Where fittings fit in a piping package

Fittings connect valves, pumps, tanks, dosing accessories, instruments, and piping into a workable system. In corrosion-resistant projects, installation problems often come from mismatched pipe standards, connection methods, gasket materials, flange drilling, thread standards, or missing adapters rather than from the valve body itself.

Fittings should be reviewed as part of the complete piping package. The material, pressure rating, temperature boundary, chemical exposure, installation method, maintenance access, and document requirements should match the valve and pipe system.

## Typical service situations

| Service area | Why fittings are considered | Review points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valve installation | Connect valves to existing or new piping | End connection, face-to-face allowance, flange standard, gasket |
| Chemical dosing packages | Build compact pipework around pumps, tanks, columns, and injection points | Small-bore fittings, tubing adapters, unions, chemical compatibility |
| Project BOM supply | Quote multiple line items as a coordinated package | Quantity, tagging, packing, drawings, material consistency |
| Maintenance and replacement | Match an existing piping system without field modification | Pipe standard, thread/flange type, old-to-new transition details |

## Selection notes

Do not treat fittings as generic accessories. In corrosive service, the fitting material must be compatible with the same media and temperature conditions as the valve and pipe. Gaskets, O-rings, fasteners, strainers, and adapters can become weak points if they are not reviewed together.

Connection standards should be confirmed early. Socket, threaded, flanged, union, butt-fusion, compression, and transition connections may require different tools, installation practices, and compatible pipe standards. A correct material with the wrong connection is still the wrong item.

For BOM-based inquiries, group the fittings by line, material, size, and connection where possible. This helps reduce quotation ambiguity and makes packing, tagging, and submittal coordination easier.

## Common documents to request

Depending on project scope, request:

- Product datasheets or catalog pages
- Dimensional drawings for key fittings
- Material information and gasket details
- Pressure-temperature information by product series
- Installation or joining instructions
- Packing list or BOM confirmation
- Inspection or material documentation if required

## RFQ checklist

To prepare a more reviewable inquiry, include:

- Fitting type and quantity
- Size, pipe standard, and connection method
- Material preference and chemical exposure
- Pressure, temperature, and operating environment
- Gasket, O-ring, bolt, nut, washer, or fastener requirements
- Whether the request is for a replacement item, valve package, or project BOM
- Drawings, line list, isometric sketch, or BOM reference if available
- Destination, packaging expectations, and target documents

Final material, pressure-temperature limit, connection compatibility, and installation details should be confirmed against the selected product series and project piping standard.
