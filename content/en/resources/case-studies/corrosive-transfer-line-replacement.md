A replacement request may start with a photo, an old tag, and a nominal valve size. Those references help identify the existing installation, but they do not prove that a visually similar valve will fit the line or suit the current service.

This representative case illustrates the review method and does not claim a completed customer replacement.

## Replacement evidence

The available information included an old valve photograph, flange-to-flange measurement, actuator voltage, and the statement that the line handled a corrosive chemical.

Before recommending a direction, the review requested:

- Chemical name, concentration, temperature, pressure, and cleaning exposure
- Existing body, seat, seal, shaft, and fastener materials
- Pipe and flange standard, nominal size, drilling, and face-to-face requirement
- Valve function, operating frequency, fail position, and actuator controls
- Installation orientation, clearance, access, and outdoor conditions
- Existing drawings, data sheets, maintenance records, and failure observations

## Fit and duty were separated

Dimensional interchangeability and process suitability were treated as two separate checks. A valve can match the available space but use unsuitable wetted materials; it can also suit the media but require piping modification because its face-to-face dimension or connection standard differs.

## Quotation notes

The revised quotation scope would identify confirmed dimensions, assumed conditions, proposed construction, actuator and accessory requirements, excluded field modifications, and documents required for approval.

That structure makes the remaining uncertainty visible. Final approval would depend on verified line data, dimensional drawings, selected product limits, and the project's replacement procedure.
