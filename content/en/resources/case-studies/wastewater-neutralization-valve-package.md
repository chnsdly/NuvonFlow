## Scenario

This representative scenario involves a wastewater neutralization area with acid, alkali, transfer, and drain lines. The project required isolation valves, check valves, compatible fittings, and a basic document package, but the early RFQ mixed product names, pipe sizes, and partial BOM notes without clear service conditions.

## Initial RFQ gaps

- Media concentration was missing for several acid and alkali lines
- Some line items listed pipe size but not connection standard
- Check valve orientation and pump discharge conditions were unclear
- Several fittings were required for installation but not listed in the BOM
- The customer needed datasheets and packing information but had not linked documents to line items

## Organized scope

| Line group | Review focus |
| --- | --- |
| Acid dosing and transfer | Material direction, seal compatibility, pump protection, injection or discharge details |
| Alkali neutralization | Temperature, concentration, check protection, flushing, and maintenance isolation |
| Wastewater transfer | Solids, intermittent flow, drain points, and maintenance access |
| Fittings and adapters | Pipe standard, connection transitions, gaskets, fasteners, and spare parts |
| Documents | Datasheets, basic dimensional notes, material declarations, packing list, and revision control |

## Practical lesson

For wastewater chemical areas, a clearer RFQ often comes from grouping the BOM by service rather than by product name alone. A line item called “valve” becomes much more reviewable when it is tied to media, function, connection, pressure, quantity, and document needs.

## What to prepare for a similar request

Share the pH range, chemical concentration, suspended solids, pump data, pipe material, connection standard, line orientation, maintenance access, and document list. If the request includes both valves and fittings, keep them in the same service group so installation assumptions are easier to check.
