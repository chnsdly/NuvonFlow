“Ball valve,” “butterfly valve,” or “check valve” describes a product family, not a complete selection. Products with the same general name can differ in body construction, wetted materials, seals, pressure-temperature limits, connection standards, operating torque, and maintenance requirements.

## Begin with the required function

First define what the valve must do:

- Isolate a line
- Prevent reverse flow
- Support throttling or frequent adjustment
- Handle solids, slurry, crystallization, or off-gassing
- Operate manually or through an actuator

The function narrows the product family, but it still does not complete the selection.

## Add the operating boundaries

Media name alone is rarely enough. Concentration, impurities, temperature, pressure differential, vacuum, cleaning fluids, exposure time, and intermittent drying can change the material and seal discussion.

Installation conditions matter as well. Pipe standard, orientation, available space, actuator access, outdoor exposure, and maintenance method can eliminate an otherwise suitable option.

## Treat the product name as the start

A useful RFQ can begin with a preferred valve family, provided it also records the actual duty and identifies unknown information. The product name creates a starting point; the operating conditions create the reviewable decision.

Final construction and limits should be confirmed against the selected manufacturer's current data and the project's approval requirements.
