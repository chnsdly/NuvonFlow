A valve may be described by its body material, but corrosion-resistant performance depends on the complete construction. Seats, O-rings, diaphragms, liners, balls, stems, shafts, bearings, fasteners, and accessories can introduce different materials and different exposure conditions.

## Map the wetted construction

Material review should identify every component that contacts the process fluid during normal operation, startup, shutdown, cleaning, leakage, or maintenance. Static and dynamic seals may see different stress, movement, and permeation conditions even when exposed to the same chemical.

## Include non-wetted components where relevant

External fasteners, actuator brackets, springs, coatings, cable entries, and enclosures may not contact the process fluid directly, but they can be exposed to splash, vapor, washdown, weather, or corrosive atmosphere.

## Review the system interfaces

The valve does not operate alone. Gaskets, flange adapters, unions, tubing, injection fittings, strainers, instruments, and pump accessories can become the limiting components if their materials or connection standards are not reviewed with the valve.

## Record conditions, not just a material preference

A useful RFQ records the chemical, concentration, impurities, temperature, pressure, cleaning method, exposure pattern, and installation environment. A preferred material can be listed, but final suitability should be confirmed for the complete selected construction and actual service.
