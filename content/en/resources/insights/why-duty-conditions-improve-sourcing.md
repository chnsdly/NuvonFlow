When a buyer sends only a product name, the supplier must guess the media, pressure, connection, material, and document scope. Those assumptions can make the first price fast but weak.

Duty conditions improve sourcing because they move the conversation from catalog matching to project fit. They also make it easier to identify which documents, tests, drawings, and spare parts should be included before the order is placed.

## Assumptions become visible

A request for “PVDF ball valves” may still leave open questions about concentration, temperature, pipe standard, pressure, actuator needs, spare seals, and project documents. Listing the duty conditions turns those hidden assumptions into visible review points.

## The first quotation becomes easier to compare

When each quotation line includes function, material direction, connection, quantity, and documents, the buyer can compare scope instead of comparing price alone. This matters when several suppliers use similar product names but different accessories, gaskets, or document packages.

## Clarification gets shorter

Good duty conditions do not eliminate every question. They make questions sharper: one supplier may need to confirm elastomer preference, another may need pump data, and another may need the project document register. That is far better than restarting the RFQ from the beginning.

## A simple format works

For each line item, prepare media, concentration, temperature, pressure, size, connection, valve function, operation mode, quantity, destination, and document requirements. If the request is based on a BOM, add service notes and tag numbers when available.
