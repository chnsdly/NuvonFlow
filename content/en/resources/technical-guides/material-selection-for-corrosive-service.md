## Start with the complete fluid picture

Material selection should begin with the media name, concentration, temperature, pressure, exposure time, solids content, and cleaning method. A chemical name without concentration or temperature is not enough for a reliable review.

If the line sees more than one chemical, list all credible exposures. Many failures happen during cleaning, flushing, startup, shutdown, intermittent drying, or an occasional batch condition rather than during the normal operating step.

## Review every wetted part

The body material is only one part of the decision. Seats, seals, diaphragms, liners, gaskets, fasteners, tubing, injection accessories, and adapters may all contact the process fluid or the surrounding environment.

| Component area | Questions to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Valve body or lining | Is the service clean, abrasive, oxidizing, solvent-based, hot, outdoor, or intermittent? |
| Seats, seals, and diaphragms | Which elastomers or soft materials are exposed to the media, cleaning fluid, and temperature? |
| Gaskets and fasteners | Are flange materials, bolting, and external corrosion conditions part of the review? |
| Fittings and tubing | Do adapters, unions, injection quills, hoses, or small-bore parts use the same compatible material direction? |

## Watch for combined risks

High temperature, oxidizers, chlorides, solvents, abrasion, vacuum, UV exposure, and intermittent drying can change the recommendation. A material that looks acceptable for one factor may be unsuitable when several factors appear together.

Examples of combined risks include oxidizing chemicals at elevated temperature, solids in a chemically aggressive slurry, outdoor chemical dosing lines, or mixed cleaning chemicals used on a normally clean process line.

## Convert uncertainty into questions

When compatibility is unclear, the best output is a short list of assumptions and clarification questions. That keeps the RFQ transparent and prevents material decisions from being hidden inside a price line.

## RFQ checklist

- Media name, concentration, impurities, and pH range if relevant
- Normal and maximum temperature
- Normal, design, and possible surge pressure
- Continuous, intermittent, batch, flushing, or cleaning exposure
- Solids, abrasion, crystallization, off-gassing, or vacuum conditions
- Preferred or customer-approved materials
- Required documents, declarations, inspection notes, or standards

Material selection should be confirmed before order placement and again whenever the process media, temperature, cleaning method, or project standard changes.
