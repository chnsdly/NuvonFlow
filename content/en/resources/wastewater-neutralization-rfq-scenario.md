This is a representative planning scenario, not a disclosed customer project or a claim about delivered performance. Its purpose is to show how project information can be made reviewable before quotation.

## Starting request

A wastewater neutralization area needs isolation valves, check protection, fittings, and several dosing accessories. The first list contains product names, pipe sizes, and quantities, but it mixes acid, alkali, transfer, drain, and flushing duties without identifying which line each item serves.

The list also omits concentration, temperature, pressure, pipe material, connection standard, actuator needs, tag references, and document requirements.

## Why the list is difficult to quote

A supplier could fill the gaps with assumptions, but different assumptions would make quotations hard to compare. A generic “valve” line cannot show whether the item is for isolation, throttling, non-return protection, or dosing control. The same body material may not be the deciding factor for every service because seats, seals, diaphragms, gaskets, and accessories also require review.

## A more reviewable structure

Group the request by service before grouping it by product:

| Service group | Information to add |
| --- | --- |
| Acid and alkali dosing | Chemical, concentration, flow, pump data, tubing or pipe interface, and control function |
| Chemical transfer | Source and destination, normal and design limits, isolation points, drainage, and flushing |
| Neutralization tank | Mixing conditions, pH range, solids, valve position, cycle frequency, and maintenance access |
| Drain and sampling | Exposure frequency, residual chemicals, connection, safe discharge, and operator access |

For each BOM line, add product function, size, connection, preferred materials, quantity, tag, duty note, and required document status.

## Documents and open questions

The initial RFQ should identify whether datasheets, drawings, IOM manuals, declarations, inspection records, or packing notes are required. Unknown items should remain visible as questions rather than being silently converted into quotation assumptions.

## Practical outcome

The organized scope does not make the final selection automatic. It makes the next review more efficient: product-family directions can be compared against the same service conditions, material uncertainties are visible, and document requirements enter the quotation before they affect approval or shipment.
