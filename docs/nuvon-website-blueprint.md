# Nuvon Website Blueprint

## 1. Website Role

The Nuvon Flow Control website must be built as an **engineering review and RFQ conversion system**, not as a simple company display website.

The business focus is anticorrosive thermoplastic valves and adjacent flow control products for:

* Industrial water treatment
* Industrial wastewater treatment
* Chemical dosing and injection
* Corrosive fluid transfer
* Hazardous chemical handling
* Double containment and leak detection systems

The website must help customers answer three questions:

1. Is this product suitable for my operating conditions?
2. Does this supplier understand material compatibility, media, temperature, pressure, and compliance boundaries?
3. Can I submit duty conditions, request documents, or move into an RFQ process?

Every major page should support engineering review, product fit evaluation, documentation request, and inquiry conversion.

## 2. Business Positioning

Nuvon should not be positioned as a self-owned factory, global leading manufacturer, or factory-direct supplier unless there is verifiable evidence in the repository.

Use this positioning:

> An application-focused anticorrosive flow control sourcing and support partner for industrial water treatment, industrial wastewater, and chemical fluid handling.

The website should build trust through:

* Application understanding
* Material and valve type matching support
* Documentation and submittal support
* Project communication support
* Coordination with selected manufacturing partners
* Clear explanation of product scope, lead time, warranty, and risk boundaries

Nuvon’s trust foundation is not factory identity. It is **engineering review capability and supply coordination capability**.

## 3. Target Customers

The website should prioritize professional B2B customers who can bring repeat orders, project-based demand, and long-term account value.

### Primary Customers

Priority should be given to:

* OEMs
* Skid builders
* System integrators

These customers usually integrate valves, fittings, actuators, instruments, and control components into larger systems.

They care about:

* Material suitability
* Temperature and pressure limits
* Drawings, manuals, datasheets, and certification notes
* Stable supply
* Alternative product selection
* BOM and equipment design support

This customer group best matches Nuvon’s early-stage capability boundary.

### Secondary Customers

Secondary customers include:

* Specialized distributors
* Regional channel partners

These customers are valuable when Nuvon can provide:

* Stable quotation process
* Clear lead time
* Warranty policy
* Return and claim process
* Technical documentation
* Reasonably complete product families

### Opportunity Customers

End industrial users may be served when there is a suitable opportunity, especially for maintenance and replacement needs in:

* Surface treatment
* Industrial wastewater
* Chemical dosing
* Corrosive media transfer

However, end users should not be the main early-stage brand foundation because site responsibility and after-sales pressure are more direct.

### Lower Priority Customers

Do not make the early website mainly target:

* Municipal owners
* Large EPC companies
* Design institutes with strict approved vendor lists

These customers usually require stronger evidence, including product-level certifications, project references, formal approval records, compliance documentation, and long-term supply history.

## 4. Customer Buying Logic

The website must be organized around how professional customers actually evaluate anticorrosive valves.

Typical buying logic:

1. Media compatibility
2. Material suitability
3. Temperature and pressure limits
4. Valve type suitability
5. Availability of reviewable documents
6. Project or market compliance requirements
7. Lead time, warranty, and after-sales control
8. Price comparison

Do not rely on vague claims such as “high quality”, “professional supplier”, or “rich experience”.

Instead, pages should explain:

* Which applications the product is suitable for
* Which operating boundaries require review
* What duty conditions the customer should submit
* Which documents can be requested
* Which details are subject to project verification
* Which certifications or documents are confirmed by product series

## 5. Product Strategy

Do not build a large SKU catalog too early.

At the early stage, build **product family pages**. Each product page should work as an application-fit and engineering-review page.

Priority product families:

* Ball Valves
* Butterfly Valves
* Diaphragm Valves
* Check Valves
* Pressure Control & Chemfeed
* Actuators
* Fittings
* Double Containment
* Leak Detection

Each product family page must answer:

1. What is this product used for?
2. Which applications and media is it typically suitable for?
3. What operating limits or selection risks should be reviewed?
4. What information should the customer prepare before requesting a quote?
5. What related documents can be requested?

General product positioning guidance:

* Ball valves are typically suitable for clean-fluid on/off service.
* Butterfly valves are typically suitable for larger-diameter on/off or throttling service.
* Diaphragm valves are often relevant for aggressive or off-gassing media.
* Check valves are used to prevent backflow.
* Pressure control and chemfeed products support dosing, back pressure, pressure stabilization, and injection systems.
* Double containment and leak detection are relevant for hazardous media and secondary containment requirements.

Product pages should not be simple product lists. They should help the customer judge whether the product may fit the application and whether further review is needed.

## 6. Website Structure

The main website structure should follow the customer decision path, not a company-introduction-first structure.

Primary navigation:

1. Products
2. Applications
3. Project Support
4. Resources
5. About

Global conversion pages:

* Request a Quote
* Submit Duty Conditions
* Contact
* Document Request

Role of each conversion page:

* `Request a Quote`: for RFQ and commercial inquiry.
* `Submit Duty Conditions`: for complex media, temperature, pressure, material, and application review.
* `Contact`: for low-friction communication through email, phone, WhatsApp, or simple message.
* `Document Request`: for requesting datasheets, submittals, CAD/BIM files, certificates, or other technical documents.

## 7. Homepage Requirements

The homepage must answer four questions:

1. Who is Nuvon?
2. What does Nuvon provide?
3. Which industries and operating conditions does Nuvon serve?
4. What should the customer do next?

Recommended homepage structure:

1. Hero section with clear positioning
2. Product family entry points
3. Application entry points
4. Engineering support and documentation support
5. Trust and capability section
6. RFQ or duty condition CTA

Do not build the homepage as a slogan-heavy visual shell. Critical business and SEO content must be visible in HTML.

## 8. Products Section

The Products section answers:

> Can this product be used for my application?

Product pages should help customers evaluate:

* Suitable media
* Pressure and temperature range
* Connection types
* System position
* Need for actuator, fittings, containment, or leak detection
* Whether duty condition review is required

Each product page should include:

* Product definition
* Typical applications
* Relevant media and operating conditions
* Core materials
* Key specification factors
* Related documents
* RFQ or duty condition CTA

## 9. Applications Section

The Applications section answers:

> What should I choose for my operating scenario?

Application pages must be organized by customer use case, not by product category.

Priority application pages:

* Industrial Water Treatment
* Industrial Wastewater Treatment
* Chemical Dosing & Injection
* Corrosive Chemical Transfer
* Chemical Processing

Application pages should translate field problems into selection questions.

Each application page should explain:

* Typical system context
* Common corrosion or process challenges
* Relevant media, temperature, pressure, and material concerns
* Recommended product families
* Selection reminders
* When material compatibility review is needed
* When containment or leak detection may be needed
* What information to prepare before inquiry
* Related resources and RFQ CTA

Application pages must not simply repeat product pages. They must start from the customer’s operating condition.

Recommended structure:

1. Application overview
2. Typical operating challenges
3. Media, corrosion, pressure, and temperature factors
4. Relevant product families
5. Selection reminders
6. Related documents or guides
7. RFQ or duty condition CTA

## 10. Project Support Section

Project Support is a key trust-building section. Do not place all trust content only on the About page.

Recommended Project Support topics:

* Submittal & Documentation
* Material Compatibility & Operating Limits
* Compliance, Delivery & Warranty

These pages should answer:

* What documents can be requested?
* How are material compatibility and operating limits reviewed?
* Which details are subject to product series or project confirmation?
* How are compliance, lead time, warranty, after-sales, and RMA handled?

Material compatibility pages must use careful language.

Do not present compatibility tables as guarantees. They should be framed as initial guidance only.

Compatibility review must consider:

* Media
* Concentration
* Temperature
* Pressure
* Stress
* Exposure time
* Vibration
* Installation condition
* Project requirements

Do not claim that a material is always suitable for a chemical without project-specific verification.

## 11. Resources Section

Resources should not be a generic news center.

It should be a technical guide and support content hub that connects search-driven users to products, applications, project support, and RFQ conversion.

Initial guide topics:

* How to Choose Valve Type for Corrosive Service
* How to Prepare RFQ and Submittal Inputs

Resources can be divided into two content types:

### Guides

Guides explain technical selection questions, such as:

* How to choose PVC, CPVC, PP, or PVDF valves
* How to compare ball valves, butterfly valves, and diaphragm valves
* What parameters matter when selecting valves for corrosive service

### Support

Support content provides document-oriented help, such as:

* Datasheets
* Certificates
* Installation guides
* Material compatibility notes
* Document request pages

Do not mix Guides and Support pages without a clear reason.

## 12. About Section

The About page should explain identity clearly and honestly.

It should answer:

* Who Nuvon is
* Which customers Nuvon serves
* Which applications Nuvon focuses on
* How Nuvon supports projects
* What Nuvon does not claim without evidence
* How customers can contact Nuvon or submit requirements

The About page should emphasize:

* Application understanding
* Supply coordination
* Documentation support
* Project response capability

Do not claim unsupported facts, including:

* Factory area
* Annual production capacity
* Certification numbers
* Major customer names
* Export country count
* Long company history
* Self-owned factory

Do not fabricate evidence.

## 13. Conversion Strategy

Nuvon should not be built as an e-commerce website or a public-price catalog.

Anticorrosive valves and related chemical handling products require review before purchase.

Before quoting or selecting products, customers may need to confirm:

* Media compatibility
* Material suitability
* Temperature and pressure limits
* Documentation availability
* Lead time
* Compliance requirements

Use two levels of conversion paths.

### Primary Conversion Paths

* Request a Quote
* Submit Duty Conditions
* Request Selection Review

### Secondary Conversion Paths

* Request Submittal
* Request CAD/BIM
* Request Material Check
* Ask About Compliance and Lead Time

Use two form types:

| Form Type                          | Where to Use                                        | Purpose                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Detailed RFQ / Duty Condition Form | Product pages, application pages, high-intent pages | Collect media, temperature, pressure, size, connection, quantity, and project stage |
| Lightweight Contact Form           | About, Contact, Guide pages                         | Reduce friction for early-stage users                                               |

The website must support both high-intent inquiries and early-stage technical research users.

## 14. Trust System

Trust must be built through reviewable evidence, clear boundaries, compliance awareness, and traceable support.

### Application Boundary

Clearly state that Nuvon focuses on:

* Industrial water treatment
* Industrial wastewater
* Chemical dosing
* Chemical fluid handling
* Corrosive media conveyance
* Double containment

A clear boundary is more credible than a broad “all valves supplier” claim.

### Material and Duty Condition Matching

Website content should be organized around:

* Media
* Concentration
* Temperature
* Pressure
* Size
* Connection
* Valve type
* Actuation method
* Containment requirement
* Target market compliance

These details are more persuasive than generic quality claims.

### Documentation Capability

Even if documentation is incomplete on Day 1, the website should reserve a clear documentation system.

Use careful language such as:

* Available on request
* By product series
* Subject to verification
* Subject to project confirmation

Important document types:

* Chemical resistance information
* Pressure / temperature data
* Datasheets
* IOM documents
* Submittals
* CAD / BIM files
* Certifications
* Warranty information
* Support contact

### Supply Chain Control

If Nuvon is not a self-owned factory, do not use “our factory” as the trust base.

Prefer language such as:

* Selected manufacturing partners
* Verified partner-factory network
* Documented supply
* Material and application matching support

The website should explain how Nuvon manages:

* Partner factory selection
* Material and product consistency
* Lead time confirmation
* Warranty handling
* Returns and claims
* Consistency between documents and delivered products

## 15. Language Boundaries

Avoid the following claims unless verified evidence exists in the repository:

* manufacturer
* factory direct
* our factory
* self-owned plant
* all valves supplier
* lowest price
* global leading
* fully certified
* drinking water approved
* one-stop solution
* 20+ years experience

Preferred language:

* selected manufacturing partners
* application-focused support
* documented supply
* corrosion-resistant solutions for water and chemical handling
* sourcing and support partner
* verified partner-factory network
* material and application matching support

The website should stay close to the real capability boundary. Accurate positioning builds stronger long-term trust than exaggerated claims.

## 16. Content and Visual Direction

The website should feel:

> Engineering-focused, scannable, low in slogan density, and evidence-driven.

Content and design should follow these rules:

* Use fewer vague brand slogans.
* Avoid purely decorative hero sections.
* Prefer application and system images over unsupported factory images.
* Use tables, comparison blocks, notes, document modules, and selection reminders.
* Structure pages around “problem → evidence → action”.
* Keep the same core information available on both desktop and mobile.
* Place risk notes close to the relevant claim or recommendation.

After reading a page, customers should know:

* Which duty conditions to submit
* Which documents to request
* Which boundaries to confirm
* Which lead time or compliance questions to ask

## 17. Technical Architecture

Recommended architecture:

> Hugo multilingual static website + Cloudflare Pages hosting + Pages Functions for limited form handling.

Responsibilities:

* Hugo handles content, pages, multilingual structure, templates, image processing, and SEO.
* Cloudflare Pages handles deployment, preview, and production publishing.
* Pages Functions handle RFQ, Contact, Duty Condition, and Document Request form submissions.

Do not introduce complex dynamic systems too early.

The first-stage website goal is:

* Stable
* Clear
* Maintainable
* Indexable
* Easy to extend

## 18. SEO Requirements

SEO must be implemented as a complete system, not only as title and description.

Core SEO requirements:

* Unique `<title>` for each page
* Page-level meta description
* Self canonical
* Correct `html lang`
* Valid hreflang for equivalent multilingual pages
* HTML breadcrumb
* BreadcrumbList JSON-LD
* Organization JSON-LD on homepage
* Product JSON-LD on product pages where appropriate
* Article JSON-LD on guide pages
* Custom sitemap
* Custom robots.txt
* Noindex for preview or non-production environments where appropriate

SEO content must be visible in generated HTML and must not depend on client-side JavaScript rendering.

## 19. SCSS Requirements

Recommended SCSS structure:

```txt
assets/scss/
├─ abstracts/
├─ base/
├─ layout/
├─ components/
├─ pages/
├─ utilities/
├─ content/
└─ main.scss
```

SCSS principles:

* Keep only one main entry file: `main.scss`.
* Prefer reusable components over page-by-page styling.
* Use `pages` only for limited page-specific overrides.
* Add prose / rich text styles for guide and support long-form content.
* Use BEM naming.
* Avoid nested element naming such as `block__element__subelement`.
* Prefer design tokens and shared spacing values over scattered hard-coded values.

## 20. Implementation Priority

Build the website in this order:

1. Foundation: Hugo structure, layout, SCSS tokens, base templates, SEO partials.
2. Core navigation: Products, Applications, Project Support, Resources, About.
3. Conversion pages: Request a Quote, Submit Duty Conditions, Contact, Document Request.
4. Product family pages.
5. Application pages.
6. Project Support pages.
7. Resource guides.
8. Documentation request system and form handling.
9. Multilingual expansion.
10. Structured data refinement and internal linking improvement.

Do not build too many low-quality pages at once. Build fewer pages with stronger structure, clearer intent, and better conversion paths.
