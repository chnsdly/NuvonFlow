Start with the service conditions that affect selection

Corrosion-resistant valves are rarely selected by product name alone. Material, seal, pressure class, connection, actuation, installation position, and document expectations all need to be visible before a quote can be reviewed.

  • Media compatibility review

    Chemical name, concentration, temperature, exposure time, solids, cleaning method, and elastomer contact should be checked before material decisions are treated as final.

  • Product family matching

    Ball, butterfly, diaphragm, check, pressure control, chemfeed, actuator, and fitting pages help narrow the component family before model-level quotation.

  • Submittal-ready communication

    Datasheets, drawings, IOM files, declarations, inspection notes, and revision-controlled project documents can be included in the quotation scope when required.

RFQ Inputs

Turn operating conditions into a reviewable quotation scope

Share the media, concentration, temperature, pressure, connection, actuation, and target documents so the product family, material direction, and quotation scope can be narrowed faster.

Media and concentration
Chemical name, concentration, corrosion, oxidizers, solids, and cleaning fluids
Temperature and pressure
Operating and design limits, vacuum, pressure differential, and cycle frequency
Connection and actuation
End standard, installation direction, manual, pneumatic, or electric operation
Documents and delivery
Datasheets, drawings, IOM files, declarations, and inspection requirements

A practical review path before quotation

Whether the request is one valve, a dosing line, a replacement item, or a project bill of materials, the first response is stronger when the duty conditions are organized.

  1. Send the duty

    Share media, concentration, temperature, pressure, flow, pipe size, connection standard, operation mode, and installation position.

  2. Narrow the fit

    Review valve function, wetted material, seal set, pressure class, end connection, actuator mode, and maintenance access.

  3. Define the RFQ scope

    Confirm quantity, destination, project stage, drawing needs, inspection expectations, spare parts, and packaging requirements.

  4. Prepare review documents

    Coordinate datasheets, drawings, manuals, declarations, test records, and revision-controlled submittals when the project requires them.
