Product Details

Where actuators fit in a valve package

Actuators convert manual valves into remotely operated or automated valve packages. They are commonly used on ball valves, butterfly valves, diaphragm valves, and selected pressure control or process assemblies where the plant needs remote open/close operation, interlock, sequencing, feedback, or modulating control.

Actuator selection should begin with the valve and duty. A valve that operates easily by hand may still require a larger actuator because of pressure differential, seat load, media behavior, temperature, cycle frequency, or safety factor requirements.

Typical service situations

Service area Why actuators are considered Review points
Automated isolation Remote open/close operation for process or utility lines Valve torque, fail position, cycle frequency, feedback requirement
Dosing and chemical skids Sequencing, interlock, or flushing logic Signal type, enclosure, accessory wiring, chemical environment
Larger butterfly valves Gear or powered operation where manual force is high Torque, operating speed, power or air supply, mounting interface
Outdoor or corrosive locations Remote operation in difficult access or harsh environments Coating, enclosure rating, fasteners, local override, cable entry

Selection notes

Pneumatic actuators are often used where compressed air is available and fast quarter-turn operation is desired. Spring-return designs can provide fail-open or fail-closed behavior, while double-acting designs require air for both directions. Solenoid valves, limit switch boxes, positioners, filter regulators, and manual overrides should be included in the scope when required.

Electric actuators are often considered where compressed air is not available or where electrical integration is preferred. Voltage, phase, frequency, control mode, duty cycle, travel time, enclosure, local controls, feedback, and manual operation should be reviewed before quotation.

For corrosive environments, the actuator is not wetted by the process fluid in normal operation, but the surrounding atmosphere can still matter. Outdoor exposure, chemical vapor, washdown, humidity, UV, and hazardous-area requirements should be stated early. Do not assume a standard enclosure is suitable for every location.

Common documents to request

Depending on project scope, request:

  • Actuator datasheet
  • Valve-actuator assembly drawing
  • Torque or sizing basis when available
  • Wiring diagram or pneumatic schematic
  • Accessory datasheets for solenoid, limit switch, positioner, or filter regulator
  • IOM or commissioning instructions
  • Inspection or functional test documentation if required

RFQ checklist

To prepare a more reviewable inquiry, include:

  • Valve type, size, material, and pressure class
  • Media, pressure differential, temperature, and operating frequency
  • Required operation: on/off, modulating, fail-open, fail-closed, or fail-in-place
  • Pneumatic air supply or electric power supply
  • Control signal and feedback requirements
  • Required accessories: solenoid, limit switch, positioner, local override, junction box, filter regulator
  • Installation environment: indoor, outdoor, corrosive, washdown, humid, or hazardous area
  • Quantity, destination, project stage, and target documents

Final actuator size, accessory scope, fail position, and enclosure suitability should be confirmed against the selected valve series and site requirements.
